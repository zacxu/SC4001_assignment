Data and common training protocol

Chronological partitions: 2017–2020 train (87,370), 2021 validation (29,057), 2022 test (26,702); 2023 is
excluded. Inputs are month, town, flat model type, storey range and six continuous variables; year, address and
station name are excluded. Category encoders and population-standardization statistics for inputs/price are fitted
on training only, then refitted on 2017–2021 for combined-data runs. Adam uses LR 0.01, batch 512,
standardized-target MSE. Selection uses seed 42, at most 20 epochs, patience 4 and RMSE improvement
0.0001 SGD.

Evidence for the selected configuration

The selected trial's epoch-6 training RMSE is SGD 34,372.58, versus validation 58,627.10. At epoch 10, training
falls to 31,678.96 while validation rises to 70,715.09. This widening gap is consistent with overfitting after the
saved checkpoint. The smaller H=16,d=2 trial has higher train/validation errors (37,147.82/65,190.26 SGD),
suggesting more limited fit under this budget; one trajectory does not isolate capacity from optimization or
temporal shift.

The full grid favors the wider hidden layer with a modest embedding dimension. Increasing d is not consistently
beneficial: the next-best H=128,d=12 configuration scores 59,688.99 SGD with 8,649 parameters, while
H=256,d=16 uses 20,577 parameters. The chosen 7,065-parameter model has the lowest validation RMSE.
Selection is on one seed and one validation year, so the ranking is not an estimate of seed stability.

Test-assisted stopping limitation

The 2022 test set selected A1's stopping checkpoint, so its final score is not an untouched-holdout estimate.
A2/A3 inherit the selected four-epoch budget. The higher test RMSE can reflect temporal distribution changes
and model error; it is not proof of overfitting alone. Validation and test scores use different fitting periods and
transforms, so their difference does not isolate the benefit of refitting. Following the updated plotting instruction,
test results appear only in final tables/comparisons.
